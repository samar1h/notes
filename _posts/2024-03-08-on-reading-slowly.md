I used to track how many books I finished each year. At some point I stopped caring about the number.

The books I remember most are the ones I read slowly — underlined, dog-eared, put down and picked back up. The books I've forgotten are the ones I rushed through to add to a list.

## Speed is not the point

Reading fast is a skill. So is reading slow. They're useful for different things. Reading a novel at the pace you'd skim a news article is like eating a meal in thirty seconds — technically done, nutritionally questionable.

## What I do now

- Read one non-fiction book at a time, no time pressure
- Allow myself to stop books that aren't working
- Re-read things I loved — second reads are almost always richer
- Take notes while reading, not after

The goal stopped being *more books* and became *more from books*. Subtle difference, completely different outcome.

> "A book is a mirror. If an ass peers into it, you can't expect an apostle to look out." — Georg Lichtenberg

Worth sitting with.
